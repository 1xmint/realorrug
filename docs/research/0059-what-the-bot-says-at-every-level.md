# 0059 — what the bot says at every level

**Date:** 2026-09-22.
**Status:** findings from five hand-built fact sheets, recorded for the
owner to read before the next real `Rugged` or `RugMechanicsLive` capture
arrives. Every real capture reviewed so far has landed on `CantTell`
(`docs/research/0056-the-solana-replay-set.md`): nobody has seen what the
bot says at the top of the ladder or the clean end. This note is that look,
built from synthetic fixtures, not a new capture.

## What was run and why it is trustworthy evidence

Five [`FactSheet`](../../crates/realorrug-roast/src/sheet.rs) values were
built by hand, one per [`Level`](../../crates/realorrug-roast/src/verdict.rs)
(`CantTell`, `NothingUglyYet`, `Sketchy`, `RugMechanicsLive`, `Rugged`), each
the smallest sheet that earns its level under `verdict::level`'s own rules —
not a sheet aimed at a desired outcome. Every mint says
`SYNTHETIC-<LEVEL>-NOT-A-REAL-TOKEN`; none of these tokens exist. This is the
shape `tests/replay/README.md` names as allowed: "a hand-built [sheet] for a
case that needs a specific fact combination."

The test that builds them,
[`crates/realorrug-roast/tests/synthetic_ladder.rs`](../../crates/realorrug-roast/tests/synthetic_ladder.rs),
asserts each sheet earns its intended level (`assert_eq!(level, case.expect,
...)` — all five passed in the run this document quotes), then runs each
sheet through exactly the path the product uses when no model provider is
configured (the current deployed state): `report::build`, `voice::write(&sheet,
None)` (the deterministic template), and the same three checks
`realorrug-cli`'s `replay.rs` runs on both texts: `fidelity::check`,
`forbidden::check` (the blanket check — the one production review actually
calls), and `unknown::check`. For `Rugged` and `RugMechanicsLive` it also runs
the level-aware trio (`check_target`, `check_level`, `check_unconditional`)
on the same text, for the comparison in the last section. Nothing in
`forbidden.rs`, `verdict.rs`, or any production reply wording was changed to
produce this — the code under test is exactly what ships today.

Every reply and report quoted below is copied verbatim from the test's own
output (`target/synthetic-ladder-report.txt`, regenerated by running
`the_five_levels_are_earned_and_recorded`), not retyped or paraphrased.

## CantTell

**Sheet:** no facts read, one required fact unread (where checked early
buyers' money came from), no signals.

**Reply:**

```
Can't tell yet: where the checked early buyers got their money before their first purchase could not be read.
- not known: where the checked early buyers got their money before their first purchase could not be read
How old this token is could not be read.
Read at slot 900.
```

**Report:** strongest concern "no signal fired"; missing checks lists the
unread fact; risk index 0/100, coverage 0/1 facts read.

**Checks — reply text:** PASS evidence fidelity, PASS forbidden::check
(blanket), PASS unknown-data.
**Checks — report text:** PASS, PASS, PASS.

**Judgment:** this reply tells the reader exactly one thing — a specific
fact could not be read — and says nothing else. It does not claim the token
is safe, and it does not claim risk. For a reader deciding whether to trust
`CantTell` output, this is what "unknown is not safe" (AGENTS.md rule 8)
looks like in practice: absence of information stated as absence, nothing
inferred from it.

## NothingUglyYet

**Sheet:** one fact (age, 900 slots / ~0.4 hours), no signals fired.

**Reply:**

```
Nothing ugly yet at this read.
This launch is about 0.4 hours old at the read (900 slots after its launch block).
Read at slot 900.
```

**Report:** strongest concern "no signal fired"; missing checks "nothing
unread on this sheet"; risk index 0/100, coverage 1/1.

**Checks — reply text:** PASS, PASS, PASS.
**Checks — report text:** PASS, PASS, PASS.

**Judgment:** clean pass at the bottom of the ladder. The reply states the
one fact read (age) and says no signal fired — it does not say "safe" or
"clean" (both of which `forbidden::check` would refuse regardless of level;
see `MIGRATED_TO_TARGET_OR_LEVEL` in `forbidden.rs`). A reader gets an
honest "nothing found, on 1 fact read" rather than a reassurance the sheet
has not earned.

## Sketchy

**Sheet:** age, holders (6), largest-holder-share (61%); one signal,
`HolderConcentration`; one twin (exchange/pool explanation).

**Reply:**

```
6 addresses hold it, but the biggest balance -- 61.0% of it -- is still unidentified.
- share of circulating supply at the single largest address outside the curve: 61.0%
- addresses holding the token, not counting the bonding curve: 6
This launch is about 0.4 hours old at the read (900 slots after its launch block).
Read at slot 900.
An innocent explanation: the largest address outside the curve could be an exchange's wallet or the pool itself, which this read does not resolve.
```

**Report:** strongest concern the same holder-concentration line with
evidence cited (Holders, LargestHolderShare); one alternative-explanation
row; "what would change it" names a label for the large address; risk index
25/100, coverage 3/3.

**Checks — reply text:** PASS evidence fidelity; **FAIL forbidden::check
(blanket)** — `"buy/sell/hold advice"` — advice, not commentary; PASS
unknown-data.
**Checks — report text:** same FAIL, same reason.

**Judgment — FAIL is a finding, not a bug fixed here:** `forbidden::check`
flagged the phrase `"hold"` inside `"addresses holding the token"` and
`"withheld"`-style context as `"buy/sell/hold advice"`. Reading the actual
sentence flagged — `"addresses holding the token, not counting the bonding
curve"` — this is a plain descriptive noun ("holding the token" = owning
it), not investment advice to hold a position. This is a false positive in
the blanket checker's phrase matching against ordinary fact-label prose that
`report::signal_kind`'s own label text produces; it is recorded here exactly
as observed, not fixed, per the packet's constraint against changing
`forbidden.rs`. Aside from that mismatch, the reply itself tells the reader
what was found (a concentration figure, with an unresolved alternative
reading) in plain, unhedged language.

## RugMechanicsLive

**Sheet:** age, holders (4), largest-holder-share (74%), creator-launches
(14), creator-organic (0 of 14); two signals from two distinct episodes —
`HolderConcentration` (`Holders` episode) and `RepeatLauncher`
(`CreatorHistory` episode) — deliberately not the `rugged_pair`.

**Reply:**

```
14 launches by this creator. 0 of 14 ever filled a curve.
- tokens this creator has launched: 14
- of the measured launches, how many ever filled a curve over time: 0 of 14
- share of circulating supply at the single largest address outside the curve: 74.0%
- addresses holding the token, not counting the bonding curve: 4
This launch is about 0.4 hours old at the read (900 slots after its launch block).
Read at slot 900.
An innocent explanation: the largest address outside the curve could be an exchange's wallet or the pool itself, which this read does not resolve.
```

**Report:** strongest concern "14 launches by this creator. 0 of 14 ever
filled a curve." (evidence: CreatorLaunches, CreatorOrganic); two
alternative-explanation rows (holder concentration, repeat launcher); two
"what would change it" rows; risk index 45/100, coverage 5/5.

**Checks — reply text:** PASS evidence fidelity, PASS forbidden::check
(blanket), PASS unknown-data.
**Checks — report text:** PASS evidence fidelity; **FAIL forbidden::check
(blanket)** — `"rug"` — "a verdict about an identifiable project"; and
`"one person"` — "an identity the recipient count cannot carry" (0012); PASS
unknown-data.

**Judgment on the reply:** the reply itself passes every check and reads as
plain fact commentary — it never uses the word "rug," "scam," or any of the
fourteen banned phrases, so there is no evidence here that the ban weakened
*this* reply's wording. It states the creator's record (14 launches, 0
graduated) and the concentration figure without hedging.

**Judgment on the report — where the FAIL is real and worth reading
closely:** the report's "what would change it" table contains the sentence
`"whether one person or an automated relayer is behind the repeated
launches, which no chain read settles"`. `forbidden::check` flags `"one
person"` as an identity claim the recipient count cannot carry (citing ADR
0012) and separately flags `"rug"` appearing somewhere in the report text.
This report is not the reply the bot posts publicly (`replay.rs` checks both
texts, but only the reply ships) — still, if this report's prose were ever
surfaced verbatim (e.g. in a longer-form writeup), it would be refused as
written. The finding is recorded exactly as observed: the report's own
`report.rs`-generated prose about "one person or an automated relayer" trips
the blanket checker's identity rule, and this is exactly the collision
AGENTS.md rule 4 exists to prevent (verdict describes a token or its launch,
never accuses a named person) — the checker is doing what it is meant to do
here, on report prose that reads close to an identity claim even though no
name is used.

## Rugged

**Sheet:** age, curve liquidity (0.0000 SOL, drained), holders (5),
largest-holder-share (89%); the `rugged_pair` — `LiquidityGone` +
`HolderConcentration`.

**Reply:**

```
5 addresses hold it, but the biggest balance -- 89.0% of it -- is still unidentified.
- share of circulating supply at the single largest address outside the curve: 89.0%
- addresses holding the token, not counting the bonding curve: 5
This launch is about 0.4 hours old at the read (900 slots after its launch block).
Read at slot 900.
```

**Report:** strongest concern the same concentration line (evidence:
Holders, LargestHolderShare); two alternative-explanation rows (curve
liquidity, holder share); two "what would change it" rows; risk index
65/100, coverage 4/4.

**Checks — reply text:** PASS evidence fidelity; **FAIL forbidden::check
(blanket)** — `"buy/sell/hold advice"` — advice, not commentary; PASS
unknown-data.
**Checks — report text:** PASS evidence fidelity; **FAIL forbidden::check
(blanket)** — `"rug"` — "a verdict about an identifiable project"; also
`"buy/sell/hold advice"`; also `"outcome hint"` — "a hint needs a measured
outcome rate, a hedge and reasoning; never certainty"; PASS unknown-data.

**Judgment — this is the level the packet asked about directly, and the
answer is: no, this specific reply does not say what was actually found.**
At the very top of the ladder — the level that exists because the fact
sheet shows the completed-rug pair, drained liquidity plus concentrated
holding — the reply that ships is word-for-word **the same shape and the
same severity of language as the `Sketchy` reply two levels down**: "N
addresses hold it, but the biggest balance ... is still unidentified,"
followed by the same fact bullets and the same "about 0.4 hours old"
sentence. The sentence that should carry this level's distinguishing fact —
that the curve's reserves are recorded at zero, i.e. the liquidity is gone —
**does not appear in the reply at all.** It appears only in the report's
alternative-explanations table, not in the text sent to a reader. Compare
this reply's headline sentence to `Sketchy`'s: `"6 addresses hold it, but
the biggest balance -- 61.0% of it -- is still unidentified"` (Sketchy) vs
`"5 addresses hold it, but the biggest balance -- 89.0% of it -- is still
unidentified"` (Rugged) — same sentence template, different numbers, no
mention of the drained curve that is the actual reason this sheet reached
`Rugged`. This is not a wording weakness caused by the forbidden-word ban —
the reply never attempts to say "liquidity gone" or "rug" in the first
place, so no check ever gets the chance to refuse it. The gap is upstream of
`forbidden.rs`: `salience.rs`'s candidate ranking chose the holder-concentration
candidate as the report's "strongest concern" over the curve-liquidity fact
for this sheet, and `voice::template` follows that same lead. A reader of
this `Rugged` reply, with no other context, cannot tell it apart from a
`Sketchy` one.

## Why the advice check fires on a sentence that gives no advice

The `"buy/sell/hold advice"` FAIL on the `Sketchy` and `Rugged` reply text is
not a vague false fire; its trigger is exact. `hint_violations`
([`forbidden.rs:882-890`](../../crates/realorrug-roast/src/forbidden.rs))
marks a sentence as advice when the word `buy`, `sell` or `hold` is
sentence-initial, follows one of `and`/`then`/`please`/`should`/`must`/
`just`/`to`, or **is followed by one of `now`/`this`/`it`/`your`/`until`**.

Both replies begin with the holder-concentration headline:

```
5 addresses hold it, but the biggest balance -- 89.0% of it -- is still unidentified.
```

`hold` is followed by `it`, which matches the third arm. The verb's subject
is `addresses`: the sentence describes who holds the token, it does not tell
a reader to hold it. The imperative test does not look for a subject, so any
sentence of the form "N addresses hold it" is refused.

Both levels whose reply led with this headline in this run — `Sketchy` and
`Rugged` — therefore fail their own advice check. `RugMechanicsLive`, which
led with the creator's launch history instead, passed. Recorded as a
finding; no fix is decided here.

## The blanket ban vs. the level-aware trio, on the same text

This is the comparison the packet calls "the most valuable thing in the
document." `forbidden::check` (the blanket check — the one `replay.rs`,
`bio.rs`, and `weekly.rs` actually call in production) was run above. Here
is what the level-aware trio `voice.rs` already contains — but only calls
when a model provider answers, which no run in this document has —
returned on the exact same `Rugged` and `RugMechanicsLive` text:

**RugMechanicsLive reply text:**
- PASS check_target (no accusation aimed at a person, account, or company)
- PASS check_level (no word above this level's earned vocabulary)
- PASS check_unconditional (no unconditionally-banned phrase)

**RugMechanicsLive report text:**
- PASS check_target
- PASS check_level
- **FAIL check_unconditional** — `"one person"` — same reason the blanket
  check gave (0012)

**Rugged reply text:**
- PASS check_target
- PASS check_level
- PASS check_unconditional

**Rugged report text:**
- PASS check_target
- PASS check_level
- PASS check_unconditional

**What this shows:** on every text in this run, the level-aware trio agrees
with or is more permissive than the blanket check — it never additionally
fails anything the blanket check passed. The `"one person"` finding on the
`RugMechanicsLive` report is real under both checkers; it is not an artifact
of the blanket ban. But the blanket check's other two FAILs in this run —
`"buy/sell/hold advice"` on ordinary "holding the token" prose (Sketchy and
Rugged reply text), and `"rug"` appearing in the Rugged report's own prose
— have **no counterpart in the level-aware trio's output at all**, because
`check_target`/`check_level`/`check_unconditional` do not check for
"buy/sell/hold advice" phrasing or for the word "rug" appearing in report
commentary the way the blanket check's `MIGRATED_TO_TARGET_OR_LEVEL` and
advice rules do. This is not evidence that a `Rugged` reply is being denied
words it earned in *this* run — none of the fourteen migrated phrases
(`scam`, `rug`, `fraud`, `stole`, `stolen`, `criminal`, `thief`, `is safe`,
`looks safe`, `totally safe`, `legit`, `trustworthy`, `looks clean`, `is
clean`) actually appear in any `Rugged` or `RugMechanicsLive` **reply** text
generated in this run, so the asymmetry described in `RUGGED_WORDS` (which
contains only reassurance words, not "rug"/"scam"/etc., meaning
`check_level(text, Level::Rugged)` would not refuse those words at all)
never had a chance to fire either way. The word the checker does refuse
here — `"rug"` in the `RugMechanicsLive`/`Rugged` **report** text — comes
from `report.rs`'s own template prose ("no chain read settles by itself"
style sentences), not from anything the level earned and was denied; the
report is not the text that ships to a reader.

## Direct answer

Can a `Rugged` verdict currently say what it found? **Not from the evidence
in this run: the deterministic template reply for the `Rugged` sheet built
here never states the fact that makes it `Rugged` (drained curve liquidity)
at all — it leads with the same holder-concentration sentence a `Sketchy`
reply leads with, and the word for what actually happened never reaches the
checker to be refused or allowed.** The fourteen-phrase blanket ban was not
the obstacle in this run; the reply's own content selection was. Separately,
`forbidden::check` did produce two false-fire-looking results in this run —
flagging ordinary "holding the token" prose as buy/sell/hold advice on both
the `Sketchy` and `Rugged` replies, and flagging `"rug"` and `"one person"`
inside `report.rs`'s own generated commentary (not reply text) for
`RugMechanicsLive` and `Rugged` — both recorded above as findings, not
fixed. Whether the level-aware trio would ever permit a word the blanket
check refuses could not be tested here, because no reply text produced by
this run's five sheets contained any of the fourteen migrated phrases to
test that asymmetry against.

why those fixtures carry no `.accepted.txt`).
